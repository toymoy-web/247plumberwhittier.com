From Dairy Farms To A City Of 100,000

Norwalk began as farm country. Of the 50 local families in the 1900 census, most worked in farming or dairying, and the area also held some of the largest sugar beet farms in Southern California. The name traces to the Anaheim Branch Railroad, whose line passed through a spot then called North-walk, and the town was recorded officially as Norwalk in 1874.

After the 1950s the farmland turned residential, and the city incorporated on August 26, 1957. The 2020 census counted about 102,800 residents in roughly 28,500 housing units. A city that size generates plumbing calls at every hour, from a failed water heater in a family home to a backed-up drain in a rented unit.
