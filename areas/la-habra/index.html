From Avocado Capital To Suburb

La Habra incorporated on January 20, 1925, with about 3,000 people. By 1928 it was the largest avocado center in Southern California, and the Hass Avocado Mother Tree grew just north in La Habra Heights until it succumbed to root rot in 2002. By 1950 the population had reached nearly 5,000.

That timeline produces a mix. Some blocks hold homes from before World War II, and others hold homes from the post-war boom. An older house may still have galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains, while a newer one next door has copper and plastic. The first question on a call is the age of the house.

Where La Habra's Water Comes From

The City of La Habra Water Division blends surface water imported by the Metropolitan Water District, which draws on the Colorado River and the State Water Project, with groundwater from Cal Domestic and three city wells. Suburban Water Systems also serves part of the area through its La Mirada system.

Imported and local water can differ in hardness, so the annual water quality report from your provider is the number to use. Harder water means faster scale in tank and tankless heaters and a stronger case for a softener. Water heater work in La Habra follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and softeners should carry NSF/ANSI 44 certification.
