Between Two Rivers: How The Ground Shapes The Pipes

Pico Rivera sits on a rich alluvial plain between the Rio Hondo and the San Gabriel River. For decades the area was farmland, with citrus, avocado, and walnut groves, and local histories describe a water table near the surface of the land.

That setting matters under a house. Soil that stays damp can hide a small supply leak for a long time, because the water soaks away and never shows at the surface. It can also add moisture around buried drain lines. If your bill creeps up and nothing looks wrong, a hidden leak is a fair suspect.

Tract Homes On Former Farmland

Pico Rivera incorporated on January 29, 1958, as the 61st city in Los Angeles County. It formed from the old communities of Pico and Rivera after World War II created a sudden demand for homes. During the 1950s, large parcels of farmland were cleared and replaced by tract homes, schools, and churches.

So the housing base is mid-century and the lots are flat. Many homes are now seven decades old, which puts original supply lines, sewer laterals, and the second or third water heater at the point where repairs start to stack up.

What To Do If You Suspect A Slab Or Yard Leak

Acting early keeps a small loss from becoming soil damage or a large bill.

  • Read the meter with everything off, then again after an hour. A change confirms water is escaping.
  • Close the main shutoff at the house and check the meter again to see if the leak is inside or on the line from the meter.
  • Feel floors for warm patches and look for damp carpet edges or a new crack along a wall.
  • Do not dig or break concrete on your own, because buried gas, electric, and water lines share many yards.
  • Photograph the meter and any damage, and keep your latest water bill handy for the call.
