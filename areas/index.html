Where The Emergency Line Applies

Service covers the city of Whittier and the communities around it. Whittier itself has six street ZIP codes, 90601 through 90606, plus four post office box ZIPs. The city is also surrounded by four unincorporated communities that share its name: West Whittier-Los Nietos, North Whittier, South Whittier, and East Whittier. Combined, they hold more people than Whittier proper.

That matters when you call. Your mailing address may say Whittier even if the home is not inside the city limits, and your water provider, permit office, and sewer agency may differ from a neighbor's across the street. The dispatcher asks for your street address, not just the ZIP, so call (562) 488-9614 if you are unsure about yours.

Nearby Cities We Serve

Each city has its own housing history, water provider, and permit office, so each gets its own page.

Santa Fe Springs

Santa Fe Springs (ZIP 90670) is home to one of California's early oil fields and a large industrial base next to homes.

La Mirada

La Mirada (ZIP 90638) is a planned community that grew from about 100 homes to more than 8,000 by 1960.

Pico Rivera

Pico Rivera (ZIP 90660) is a neighboring city to the northwest of Whittier, covered by the same emergency line.

Hacienda Heights

Hacienda Heights (ZIP 91745) sits on the slopes of the Puente Hills, where lots and foundations vary.

La Habra

La Habra (ZIP 90631) sits just east of Whittier near the Los Angeles and Orange County line.

Norwalk

Norwalk (ZIP 90650) is a former dairy town southwest of Whittier with about 102,800 residents.

Downey

Downey (ZIP 90241) is a city of more than 114,000 people that built Apollo hardware.

Montebello

Montebello (ZIP 90640) has oil-hill streets and a river edge northwest of Whittier.

Several Water Providers Share The Area

Whittier alone is served by City of Whittier Water, Suburban Water Systems, San Gabriel Valley Water Company, and Orchard Dale Water District. Each reads its own meters, sets its own backflow rules, and publishes its own annual water quality report.

Keep a recent bill handy when you call. The provider's name helps with leak detection, water line questions, and any backflow notice you may have received.

Why Local Details Change The Job

Each area has its own housing history, and that history decides what fails. Whittier's most common build decade is the 1950s, so aging supply lines and clay or iron laterals come up often. Santa Fe Springs sits on a former oil field and mixes homes with a large industrial base, so lines there serve very different loads.

La Mirada was built out quickly in the 1950s as a planned community, so many homes share the same age and the same pipe vintage. Hacienda Heights sits on the Puente Hills, where slopes and foundations vary from lot to lot. In practice, the technician asks about your foundation type, pipe age, and water provider before suggesting a fix.
