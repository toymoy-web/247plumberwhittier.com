A Planned City Built In The 1950s

La Mirada's land belonged to Andrew McNally of Rand McNally from 1888 until it was subdivided and sold in 1953. Early 1950s coverage called the new community the nation's completely planned city. By 1960, when it incorporated as Mirada Hills on March 23, it had grown from about 100 homes to more than 8,000.

That speed has a plumbing consequence you can plan around. Thousands of homes were built in the same few years with the same materials, so supply lines, drain lines, and water heaters tend to reach the end of their service lives within a similar window.

Where La Mirada's Water Comes From

Suburban Water Systems owns the La Mirada water system. It pumps groundwater from wells in the Main San Gabriel Basin and the Central Basin and adds purchased supply from other agencies. A treatment plant in the city removes inorganic color and arsenic from one well's output.

The practical point is that your provider's annual water quality report applies to your tap, and it lists hardness. Hard water speeds scale in older water heaters, so that report helps you decide how soon a replacement or a softener makes sense. Any replacement heater must be braced and permitted under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

What To Check In An Older La Mirada Home

Homeowners who stay ahead of problems pay less than those who wait for a failure.

  • Look at your water heater's data plate for the date, and plan replacement once it nears a decade.
  • Find the main shutoff and turn it once to confirm it moves.
  • Watch for the sign of a lateral problem: more than one slow drain, or a gurgle in the tub.
  • Check the bathroom and kitchen shutoffs under the fixtures for drips or stains.
  • Ask for a camera look at the sewer lateral before buying an older home.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak In La Mirada

Tract homes here often share a layout, which makes the steps below quick to follow.

Shut off the main water valve

Look for the main valve at the front of the house or in the meter box, and turn it clockwise until it stops.

Kill power to wet areas

If water reached outlets, appliances, or the garage water heater, switch off those breakers before you touch anything.

Clear the area

Lift rugs, boxes, and furniture out of the wet zone, and keep children and pets out of the room.
