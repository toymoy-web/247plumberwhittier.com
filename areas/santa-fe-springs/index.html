Plumbing Trouble Where Homes Meet Industry

An emergency plumber in Santa Fe Springs, CA 90670 handles a mix of calls that few neighboring cities share. A backed-up drain in a family kitchen and a flooded floor in a warehouse can come in on the same night, and both reach the same 24/7 line.

The city covers about nine square miles of land. Its main ZIP code holds roughly 5,800 housing units, and about 63 percent of the occupied homes are owner-occupied. Around those homes sits a large industrial base.

That mix shapes the work. Residential calls lean toward drains, water heaters, and aging supply lines. Business calls lean toward grease, floor drains, and backflow devices.

From Oil Field To Industrial Park

Santa Fe Springs earned its place in California history when Union Oil began drilling near Norwalk Boulevard in 1907, and the Union-Bell well came in as a 2,500-barrel gusher in 1921. During the 1920s the field produced as much as 345,000 barrels a day, more than Signal Hill or Huntington Beach.

That past left a city organized around industrial use, and plumbing demand follows it. Many older shops here run heavy hot water and drain loads, so the first question on a call is what the building does, because a machine shop and a bakery need different solutions.

Before We Arrive At Your Santa Fe Springs Home Or Shop

These four steps limit damage in the minutes before a technician reaches you.

Close the main valve

In a house, find the main valve near the meter or on the front wall. In a shop or warehouse, find the building isolation valve before floor drains fill.

Kill power near water

Switch off breakers for outlets and equipment close to standing water, and do not step into water around machines or panels.

Clear the area

Move people, stock, and cardboard away from the leak, and keep customers and crew out of flooded zones.
