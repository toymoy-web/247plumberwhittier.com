Oil Hills, A River Edge, And A Name From The Italian

Montebello started as farmland. Businessmen Harris Newmark and Kaspare Cohn subdivided about 1,200 acres in 1899, and the area became known for flowers, berries, fruits, and vegetables. City leaders adopted the name Montebello in 1920 on William Mulholland's suggestion, and it means beautiful mountain in Italian.

Standard Oil found oil in the Montebello hills in 1917, and that field eventually produced one-eighth of California's crude oil. Over six decades, wells replaced much of the farmland. The city covers 8.37 square miles at about 203 feet of elevation, with the Rio Hondo along its edge.

A Water System Older Than The City

The city maintains a water system that was first completed in 1900 as the Montebello Land and Water Company. The city itself incorporated on October 16, 1920, so the system came first.

That history does not tell you what is in your own walls, but it explains why provider and meter details vary around town. Keep a recent bill handy when you call, because the provider named on it matters for meter location, leak questions, and backflow notices.

A Quick Check Before You Call From Montebello

These five checks take a few minutes and help the technician arrive prepared.

  • Find the main shutoff and turn it once to confirm it moves.
  • Read the water meter, wait two hours with everything off, and read it again to see if it moved.
  • Look at your most recent bill for a jump that no habit change explains.
  • Check the water heater base and the nearest wall for moisture.
  • Note whether pressure feels different upstairs and downstairs.
