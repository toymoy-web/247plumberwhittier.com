The Pipe Between The Meter And Your House

Your water service line is the buried pipe that carries water from the meter to your house. Water line repair and replacement in Whittier deals with leaks, breaks, and worn-out sections of that single run. It is separate from the pipes inside the house, which fall under whole-home repiping.

Responsibility usually splits at the meter. The provider maintains the meter and the line on the street side, and the homeowner maintains the line from the meter to the house. Whittier is served by several providers, so check your bill to learn which one yours is.

Signs The Service Line Is Leaking

Meter running with the house off

If the leak indicator moves after the main shutoff at the house is closed, the loss is on the buried line.

Wet or sunken ground

A soggy strip of yard, a sunken patch, or unexplained green growth between the meter and house can mark a leak.

Low pressure or surging flow

A partly failed line can drop pressure at the house, especially when several fixtures run.

Higher bills with no new usage

A leak that never surfaces still passes through the meter and onto your bill.

What To Do If You Suspect A Service Line Leak

A few quick checks help you confirm the problem and limit loss.

  • Close the main shutoff at the house and read the meter. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and the house.
  • Locate the shutoff valve at the meter box. Do not force it, because it may belong to the provider.
  • Photograph the wet area and note when you first saw it.
  • Call the emergency line if water is surfacing, and ask your provider to confirm meter readings if the bill is high.
  • Avoid digging on your own. Buried gas, electric, and communication lines run through many yards.

Repair A Section Or Replace The Line

A single clean break in otherwise good pipe can be repaired. When the line is old and corroded, replacing it prevents repeat digging.

Repair a section

  • Shorter job and smaller trench.
  • Works when the pipe near the break is sound.
  • Lower cost today.
  • Leaves aged pipe in the ground.

Replace the line

  • New pipe from meter to house.
  • Ends the cycle of repeat leaks.
  • Can be done with trenchless methods in some yards.
  • Higher cost and longer job.
