Whittier Earthquake Country

What Your Plumbing Should Survive

The magnitude 5.9 Whittier Narrows earthquake struck on October 1, 1987. Shaking in Whittier reached the Severe level, the strongest recorded anywhere in that event, and the Uptown district took the worst damage.

That history has a plumbing consequence you can act on today. California Health and Safety Code 19211 requires tank-style residential water heaters to be braced, anchored, or strapped, and the California Plumbing Code calls for straps in the upper and lower thirds of the tank. A heater that tips over can tear its own water and gas lines loose.

  • Count the straps on your tank. Two are the minimum, one in the upper third and one in the lower third, fastened to wall studs or masonry.
  • Find the water main shutoff and turn it once to confirm it moves. A stuck valve during a burst pipe costs minutes you do not have.
  • Locate the gas shutoff, but leave it alone unless you smell gas. If you do, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911.
  • Ask any technician working on your water heater repair to check the strapping while the tank is accessible.

Neighborhoods

Whittier Neighborhoods And Landmarks We Cover

Plumbing problems follow the age of the house and the lay of the land, so it helps to know which part of Whittier you are calling from. These are the areas and reference points the dispatcher hears about most.

Uptown Whittier

The traditional business core around Philadelphia Street and Greenleaf Avenue, with Craftsman and Spanish Colonial Revival homes nearby. Seized shutoffs and old fixtures are common calls here.

College Hills And The Historic Districts

College Hills surrounds Whittier College, which began as Whittier Academy in 1891. Central Park and Hadley-Greenleaf lie north of Uptown, and older pipe is the usual story.

Friendly Hills, Murphy Ranch, And Leffingwell Ranch

These tracts were developed in the 1950s and 1960s east of College Avenue. Slab leaks, roots in laterals, and aging water heaters lead the calls.

Starlite Estates And The Northern Hills

Homes on slopes see more pressure change than homes on the flat. A regulator check explains many weak showers and dripping relief valves.
