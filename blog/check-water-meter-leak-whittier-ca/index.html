How to Read Your Water Meter for a Leak in Whittier, CA

A water meter never lies about water that is moving. This ten-minute test shows if you have a hidden leak and roughly where it sits.

The Short Answer

To check for a hidden leak, shut off every water-using fixture and appliance, read your meter, wait about two hours without using water, and read it again. If the numbers changed at all, water is escaping somewhere. If a small triangle or dial on the face keeps spinning with everything off, the leak is active right now.

The test costs nothing and uses only your provider's meter. It also tells you if the leak is on your side of the meter, which decides who needs to fix it. Write the numbers down each time, because a pattern over several days is stronger evidence than a single reading. Take a photo of the meter face too, since a dated image helps if your provider reviews a high bill.

Why It Is Worth Ten Minutes

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's WaterSense program reports that the average household's leaks waste more than 10,000 gallons a year, and that ten percent of homes have leaks wasting 90 gallons or more per day. Fixing easily corrected leaks can save about ten percent on a water bill.

EPA also offers a simple winter check. A family of four that uses more than 12,000 gallons a month in winter, when little water goes to lawns, likely has a serious leak. If your bills run higher than that and nobody is filling a pool, start with the meter.

The Two-Hour Meter Test

Do this when nobody will be home or using water for a couple of hours, such as a quiet morning.

  • Turn off all water use inside and out, including the dishwasher, washing machine, ice maker, and irrigation controller.
  • Find the meter, usually in a ground-level box near the curb or the edge of the property. Lift the lid carefully and brush away dirt and spider webs.
  • Look for a small leak indicator on the face, often a triangle or a spinning dial. If it moves with everything off, water is flowing.
  • Write down the full reading. Take a photo for reference.
  • Wait two hours without using any water, then read the meter again. A change means a leak.
  • Close the main shutoff at the house and look at the indicator again. If it stops, the leak is inside or on your side of the main. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house.

Units On Your Meter

Meters in Southern California are usually labeled in one of two ways.

Cubic feet

A reading in cubic feet means each unit is one cubic foot, which is about 7.5 gallons.

CCF

One CCF equals 100 cubic feet, which is about 748 gallons.

Gallons

Some meters read directly in gallons, which makes the math simple.

Your bill

Your bill shows which unit your provider uses. Match it to the meter before comparing.
