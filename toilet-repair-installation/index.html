A Toilet That Runs, Rocks, Or Leaks

Most toilet trouble falls into a few groups. Water keeps running, the bowl rocks or leaks at the base, the flush is weak, or the toilet is simply old. Toilet repair and installation in Whittier covers all of them, from a fill valve swap to a complete new toilet.

A running toilet is the most common call and the most expensive to ignore. The leak is silent, and it shows up only on the water bill.

Emergency Toilet Repair: When It Cannot Wait

A toilet is an emergency when it is overflowing, when water is spreading from the base or the supply line, or when it is the only toilet in the home and will not flush. Stop the flow first. Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise, and lift the float in the tank if water keeps rising.

If the shutoff valve is stuck or leaking, close the main valve for the house. Then call the 24/7 line and describe what you see, including whether the water is clean or dirty. A toilet that runs all night is also worth an urgent visit, since it keeps refilling the tank and wasting water.

When Several Drains Back Up, It Is Not Just The Toilet

If the toilet gurgles when a sink or shower drains, or if more than one fixture backs up together, the blockage is probably in the main line and not in the toilet. A plunger will not help in that case. Stop using water and call for emergency drain cleaning.

A single toilet that will not flush after a plunger and an auger is a local blockage or a failed part. A cracked bowl, a loose flange, or a worn wax ring can also cause leaks at the base that look like a drain problem.

Try This Before You Call

A few quick checks save a visit more often than you would expect.

  • Drop a few drops of food coloring into the tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper leaks.
  • Look at the water level in the tank. It should sit about an inch below the top of the overflow tube.
  • Close the supply valve on the wall if the toilet is overflowing or leaking, then call.
  • If the toilet is clogged and not overflowing, try a flange plunger before anything stronger.
  • Keep chemical drain cleaners out of a toilet. They can damage the bowl and harm the technician.

Repair Or Replace The Toilet

A repair is worth it for a good toilet with a single failed part. An old, cracked, or inefficient one is a different story.

Repair when

  • The bowl and tank are intact.
  • A part such as the flapper or fill valve has failed.
  • The toilet flushes well otherwise.
  • You like the model and fit.

Replace when

  • The porcelain is cracked.
  • The toilet clogs often.
  • Repairs keep recurring.
  • You want a more efficient model.

Water Use And Rebates

Current California standards cap new toilets at 1.28 gallons per flush, and older units can use several times that amount. Some water providers offer rebates for replacing older toilets, and programs differ by provider and year.

Ask your own provider before you buy, because some rebates require the model to appear on an approved list or require pre-approval. A toilet that wastes water also appears on your bill, so replacement can pay for itself over time.
