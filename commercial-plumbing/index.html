Plumbing That Keeps A Business Open

When a drain backs up in a restaurant or a supply line fails in a retail suite, every hour closed costs money. Commercial plumbing in Whittier handles repairs, maintenance, and tenant improvements for businesses, with the 24/7 emergency line answering outside opening hours.

Commercial systems differ from residential ones. Pipes are larger, runs are longer, fixtures see heavier use, and rules cover grease, backflow, and accessibility. Whittier also has a mix of older storefronts and newer suites, and each has its own plumbing history.

Common Commercial Calls

Restaurant grease and drain backups

Kitchen lines carry fats, oils, and grease that coat pipes and clog traps and interceptors.

Leaks in suites and storefronts

A supply leak in a shared wall can reach a neighbor's space and create liability.

Water heater failures

Commercial heaters serve dishwashing, restrooms, and cleaning, so a failure can force a closure.

Backflow and cross-connection tests

Irrigation, fire lines, and some fixtures need devices tested on a schedule.

Grease, Interceptors, And Local Rules

Restaurants and food preparation sites generally need a grease interceptor or trap sized to the kitchen, and the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), sets how those devices are installed. Local sewer agencies often add their own rules on fats, oils, and grease.

Ask the property manager or your city what applies at your address. Pump-out records and cleaning logs help during an inspection, and regular hydro jetting of kitchen lines reduces unplanned shutdowns.

What To Do When Plumbing Fails At Work

A few steps limit damage and protect your business while help is on the way.

  • Locate and close the main shutoff for the suite or building, and tell the property manager.
  • Move inventory, files, and electronics away from the wet area.
  • Photograph the damage and note times, since insurers and landlords will ask.
  • If sewage is involved, keep staff and customers out of the affected area.
  • Call the emergency line and give an authorized contact who can approve work.
