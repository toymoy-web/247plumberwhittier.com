Pipes Failing One Leak At A Time?

Repiping replaces the supply pipes inside your home, the lines that carry water from the point where it enters the house to every faucet and appliance. In Whittier, the question usually arises after a second or third leak, when patching starts to feel like paying for the same repair again and again.

Water that enters through the street connection is a separate topic. Our page on water line repair and replacement covers the buried line between the meter and the house, while repiping covers everything inside.

Signs The System Is Wearing Out

Repeated pinhole leaks

Small leaks in different spots usually mean the pipe wall is thin throughout, not that you had bad luck.

Low pressure at many fixtures

Mineral and rust buildup inside galvanized steel narrows the opening and starves faucets.

Brown or rusty water

Discolored water from several taps points to corrosion inside the pipes.

Blue-green stains at fittings

Green crust on copper joints is a sign of slow seepage or corrosion.

How A Repipe Is Planned And Done

Survey

The technician traces existing lines and notes the layout.

Plan

Pipe type, route, and wall openings are agreed with you.

Install

New lines go in, usually zone by zone to limit disruption.

Test and patch

Pressure tests, inspection, and drywall patching close the job.

Why Whittier Homes Come Up For Repiping

The 1950s is Whittier's most common build decade. Supply lines installed then include copper and galvanized steel, and both have been in service for roughly seven decades. At that age, the pipe wall has often thinned and leaks begin to cluster.

That history is not a prediction about your house. It is a reason to ask for a camera look at the oldest section, a pressure test, and an honest recommendation on whether a partial replacement could carry the home for decades.

Before You Agree To A Repipe

Whole-home repiping is a major project. These questions keep the decision clear.

  • Ask what evidence points to the whole system, such as leak history or a pipe sample showing thin walls.
  • Ask whether partial repiping of just the worst zone would solve the problem.
  • Ask which pipe material is proposed and why, in writing.
  • Ask how many wall openings are planned and who patches and paints them.
  • Ask who pulls the permit and when the inspection happens.
  • Ask that pipe and fittings for drinking water carry NSF/ANSI 61 certification, and that materials follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Related Work Around A Repipe

If leaks are hidden, leak location shows how widespread the problem is before you commit. A sudden failure that cannot wait is a job for pipe burst repair, and a new pipe system pairs well with a water softener to reduce scale at fixtures.
