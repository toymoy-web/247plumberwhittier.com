When A Sewer Lateral Has To Be Replaced

Sewer line replacement in Whittier swaps the failing pipe between your house and the main in the street for a new one. It is the answer when a camera shows a collapsed section, a belly that holds water, or pipe so cracked and root-filled that lining or spot repair would not last.

Not every bad sewer line needs this. Our page on lateral repair covers the lighter fixes, and replacement is what remains when those fall short. The camera footage makes that call, and you should see it before agreeing to anything.

Repair Or Replace: A Decision Guide

What the camera showsUsual conclusionReason
One crack or one root intrusion, pipe otherwise roundRepair or lining.The rest of the pipe is sound enough to keep.
Multiple cracked joints along the runReplacement.Spot repairs would leave other weak joints behind.
A sagging section that holds waterReplacement of that section or the run.Debris collects there no matter how often it is cleaned.
Collapsed or crushed pipeReplacement.A liner needs a continuous open pipe to cure inside.
Pipe flaking or thinning with ageReplacement.Brittle pipe may fail again right beside a repair.

Trenchless Or Open Trench

Two methods dominate. Which one applies depends on the pipe, the path, and what sits above it.

Trenchless replacement

  • Little digging, often at two access points.
  • Protects lawns, patios, and driveways.
  • Needs a clear path for the new pipe.
  • Not suited to every collapsed run.

Open trench

  • Works almost anywhere.
  • Lets the crew fix grade and slope directly.
  • Needs concrete, plants, or paving removed.
  • Restoration adds to the job.

Permits, 811, And The Public Right Of Way

California requires a call to 811 before digging, which connects to DigAlert in Southern California. Replacing a lateral generally needs a permit from the city's building department, and work that reaches the street or the connection at the main may need approval from the city or sanitation agency as well.

Ask who handles each step and how inspections are scheduled. A crew that manages the paperwork saves you from a stalled job and from an unpermitted line that could cause trouble at resale.

How A Replacement Job Unfolds

Plan

You and the crew agree on the camera footage, route, and method in writing.

Permit

The crew calls in utility locates and pulls the permit.

Replace

The crew removes or bursts the old lateral and lays the new pipe.

Test

An inspector checks flow, slope, and connections before backfill or closeout.

What The New Line Should Include

Modern pipe

New laterals are commonly ABS or PVC, which resist roots and corrosion at the joints.

Proper slope

The California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), sets slope so waste moves without pooling.

A cleanout

A cleanout near the house gives future cameras and cables a way in.

A clean connection

The tie-in to the main should be inspected, not buried unchecked.
