What To Do In The First Five Minutes

Burst pipe repair in Whittier starts before anyone arrives. These safety steps limit the damage.

  • Shut off the main water valve. It is often at the front of the house, near the meter, or on the garage wall where the line enters.
  • Turn off electricity to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water. If not, stay out and call.
  • Open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain remaining pressure, and flush the toilets once.
  • Move valuables, rugs, and electronics out of the wet area, and photograph everything before cleanup.
  • Call the emergency line, tell us where the water is coming from, and ask for the arrival estimate.

Why Pipes Burst Even Without A Freeze

Southern California rarely sees the hard freezes that split pipes elsewhere, yet pipes still burst. In Whittier the causes are mostly corrosion, pressure, and impact. Galvanized steel rusts from the inside, copper wears thin or develops pinholes, and a fitting that has been weeping for months can finally let go.

Pressure matters too. The California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), calls for a pressure regulator when street pressure exceeds 80 psi, and a failed or missing regulator can push old pipes past their limit. Many Whittier homes date to the 1950s, so the pipes and regulators have had decades to age.

Repair The Section Or Repipe

One burst in an old system often means the rest is close behind. The right choice depends on the pipe, not just the leak.

Repair the failed section

  • Fastest way to restore water.
  • Works when the rest of the pipe is sound.
  • Lower cost today.
  • Leaves aging pipe in place.

Repipe the system

  • Replaces pipe that is failing as a group.
  • Reduces the chance of repeat bursts.
  • Higher cost and longer job.
  • Common for old galvanized or leaking copper.

After The Pipe Is Fixed

A burst often exposes other problems. If water traveled through a wall or slab, leak testing confirms there is no second leak, and an aging system may justify pricing repiping before the next failure.
