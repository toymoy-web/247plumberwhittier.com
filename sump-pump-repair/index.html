When A Small Pump Is The Only Thing Between You And Standing Water

A sump pump sits in a pit at the lowest point of a space and pushes water out before it can rise onto the floor. When it fails during a storm, the pit overflows and water spreads across a garage, a stairwell, or a basement room within hours. Sump pump repair in Whittier is usually an urgent call for that reason.

Southern California has far fewer sump pumps than wetter regions, because most homes sit on a slab or a raised foundation with no basement. Where a pump does exist, it tends to protect a below-grade garage, a basement, a sunken stairwell, or a lot that collects runoff. Most rain here arrives in a few winter storms, so a pump can sit idle for months and then be asked to run all night.

Signs Your Sump Pump Is Failing

A pump rarely dies without warning. These signs deserve a visit before the next storm.

Running nonstop

A pump that never shuts off may have a stuck float, a failed check valve, or a pit that is receiving more water than the pump can move.

Not starting

If water rises and the pump stays silent, the float, the switch, the outlet, or the motor has failed.

Loud grinding or rattling

Noise often points to a worn motor bearing or debris caught in the intake.

Short cycling

A pump that turns on and off every few seconds usually has a bad check valve or a float set wrong.

A musty or sewage smell

A pit that stays wet or a failing ejector seal can leave an odor in the room.

Emergency Steps If The Pit Is Overflowing Right Now

Stay safe

Do not touch the pump or step into standing water if the outlet or cord is wet. Turn off the breaker first if you can reach the panel dry.

Check the plug

A tripped GFCI outlet or a loose plug is a common reason a pump stops.

Move belongings

Lift boxes, rugs, and electronics off the floor and away from the water.

Call the line

Describe the pit, the pump type, and how fast the water is rising so the right parts come with the technician.

Sump Pump Or Sewage Ejector Pump

The two look similar from a distance, but they move different water and follow different rules. Mixing them up is a costly mistake.

Sump pump

  • Moves groundwater and rainwater from a pit.
  • Handles clear water, with small solids at most.
  • Discharges to a permitted outdoor location.
  • Common in basements and below-grade garages.

Sewage ejector pump

  • Moves waste from a below-grade bathroom, laundry, or kitchen.
  • Built to pass solids.
  • Sealed basin with a vent connection.
  • Tied into the home's drain and vent system.

A Five-Minute Pump Test Before Winter Storms

Because a pump may sit idle for months, test it once before the rainy season.

  • Check that the pump is plugged into a working GFCI outlet and not an extension cord.
  • Clear any dirt, gravel, or debris from the bottom of the pit.
  • Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the float lift and the pump start.
  • Confirm the pump empties the pit and shuts off without short cycling.
  • Look at the discharge pipe outside to make sure it is open and sending water away from the foundation.
