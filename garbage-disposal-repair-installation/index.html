A Kitchen Sink That Hums, Leaks, Or Won't Drain

A garbage disposal that hums and does nothing, a sink that drains slowly, or a puddle in the cabinet under the sink all lead back to the same small appliance. Garbage disposal repair and installation in Whittier covers jams, leaks, tripped resets, and complete replacements.

The appliance is simple, but it sits in a busy spot. It is bolted to the sink flange, wired to power, connected to the dishwasher drain, and tied into the trap. A fault in any one of those connections can look like a dead disposal, so the first job is figuring out which part has failed.

Safe Checks Before You Call

Most jams can be cleared in minutes if you follow safety steps first.

  • Turn off the disposal switch and unplug the unit or switch off its breaker. Never put a hand in the disposal, even when it is off.
  • Shine a light into the drain and remove visible debris with tongs, not fingers.
  • Find the hex socket in the center of the unit's underside and work a hex key back and forth to free a jammed flywheel.
  • Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit, then restore power and test with a little water running.
  • If it still hums, trips again, or leaks, stop and call. Repeating resets can overheat the motor.

Repair Or Replace

A disposal is a modest appliance. After a few repairs, a new one often costs less than keeping the old one alive.

Repair makes sense when

  • The unit is only a few years old.
  • The problem is a jam, a reset, or a gasket.
  • The housing is intact and dry.
  • The motor spins freely when cleared.

Replace makes sense when

  • Water leaks from the housing itself.
  • The motor is burned out or seized.
  • The unit is past a decade of use.
  • You want more power for heavy use.

What A New Installation Includes

Remove

The old unit and mounting ring come out, and the flange is cleaned.

Mount

The new disposal is attached and the sink flange is sealed with putty or a gasket.

Connect

The dishwasher drain, drain trap, and electrical connection are made.

Test

The unit is run with water and checked for leaks and noise.

Keeping The Disposal Healthy

Cold running water, small food scraps, and regular use extend a disposal's life. Grease, coffee grounds in large amounts, fibrous peels, and bones do not belong in it. A kitchen line that clogs just past the disposal often needs drain unclogging and not a new disposal, and a dripping spout nearby may be a job for faucet and sink repair.
