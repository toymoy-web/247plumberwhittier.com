Warm Floor Or Water Under The Floorboards?

A floor that feels warm in one spot, a hot water bill that jumps, or water seeping up through carpet or baseboards often means a supply line is leaking beneath the concrete slab. Slab leak detection in Whittier finds the exact point so the repair stays small.

When A Slab Leak Is An Emergency

A slab leak becomes an emergency when water is coming up through flooring, seeping through concrete, or pooling along an outside wall. It also qualifies when the water bill has jumped and the meter will not stop. Water under a slab soaks flooring quickly, so these calls should not wait.

Close the main valve first. If the meter keeps running with the valve closed, the leak is probably on the line before the valve. Then call the 24/7 line, move rugs and furniture off the wet area, and keep power away from any wet outlet.

Signs Of A Slab Leak

Warm spot on the floor

A hot water line leaking under the slab can warm the concrete above it, often noticed barefoot.

Sound of running water

A faint hiss or trickle when every fixture is off suggests pressurized water escaping somewhere.

Damp carpet or lifted flooring

Moisture moving up through the slab can stain baseboards and curl laminate or wood.

Spinning meter

If the leak indicator moves with all water off and the main shutoff leads to the same result, the leak may sit beneath the floor.

Where Slab Leaks Show Up First

Homeowners describe the same handful of places again and again.

Garage edge

Dampness where the garage slab meets the house foundation can come from a supply line under the concrete.

Bedroom floor

Moisture, a warm patch, or a lifted floor edge in a bedroom can mean a hot or cold line has failed beneath it.

Driveway or patio concrete

A line from the street or to a detached space can leak under flatwork, and water may rise through a crack.

Outside pooling

Water standing near the foundation with no rain or sprinkler running is worth a meter test.

The bill alone

Sometimes the only sign is usage climbing with nothing visible. Run the meter test before assuming it is something else.

Other Lines That Run Under The Slab

Hot water lines from the water heater run under the floor in many slab homes, so a high hot water bill or a warm patch of flooring often points there. A leak on the line serving the heater is found the same way as any other pressure line.

A drain or sewer line can also leak beneath concrete, and that is a different diagnosis. It starts with a camera and belongs with sewer spot repair. If the supply system under the slab keeps failing, repiping through the attic or walls avoids cutting the floor again.

How A Slab Leak Is Found

The work begins above ground. A technician runs a meter and pressure test to prove a leak exists, and testing follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Valves are then closed one at a time to learn which line is losing water. Hot and cold lines are tested separately, since a warm spot usually points to the hot side.

Listening equipment follows. Sensitive microphones pick up the sound of water escaping and mark the loudest point on the floor. When the sound is faint or confusing, a tracer gas test or a line-by-line pressure check confirms the location before anything is cut.

In many Southern California homes of that era, copper supply lines run in or under the slab. Copper can develop pinholes from corrosion or from rubbing against concrete over decades. Whittier has both slab and raised-foundation homes, so the first question on the phone is which one yours is.

What Insurance Tends To Cover

Policies vary, and slab leaks are a place where wording matters. Many cover damage caused by a sudden leak, and some cover the cost of reaching the pipe, which can be the largest part of the bill. Most do not cover the pipe itself or a slow leak that went unaddressed.

Photograph the damage, keep any detection report, and call your insurer before approving floor demolition. Written proof of where the leak was found helps a claim.
