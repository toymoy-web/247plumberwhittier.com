Replacing A Tub Or Building A Walk-In Shower

A shower or bathtub installation in Whittier usually begins with one of three goals: replacing a worn tub, converting a tub into a walk-in shower, or adding a shower where none exists. The visible part is tile and fixtures. The part that decides whether it lasts is the plumbing behind the wall.

Many Whittier bathrooms date to the 1950s and are compact, with a standard tub-shower combination along one wall. That layout affects what fits, how the valve can be reached, and where the drain runs, so the first visit includes measuring the room and looking at the access panel.

Signs It's Time For A New Tub Or Shower

Soft or stained surround

Spongy tile or a stained wall below the tub may mean water has been escaping behind it.

A valve that drips or cannot hold temperature

Worn cartridges and old mixing valves can swing from cold to scalding.

Cracked or chipped tub surface

A damaged enamel or fiberglass surface can leak through hairline cracks.

Slow drain after cleaning

A drain that clogs repeatedly may have a rough trap, poor slope, or an undersized pipe.

Code Details That Affect A Shower

The California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), calls for shower valves of the pressure-balancing or thermostatic type, so a flush elsewhere does not scald the person in the shower. California also limits new showerheads to 1.8 gallons per minute.

A shower floor needs a proper waterproof membrane, a drain at the lowest point, and a slope toward it. Ask who is responsible for the membrane and for flood testing it, because the plumber, the tile setter, and the general contractor often split the job.

Questions To Ask Before You Start

A bath remodel crosses trades. These questions keep the plumbing on track.

  • Who sets and tests the valve and the drain, and when is the plumbing inspected?
  • What valve type is proposed, and is it pressure-balancing or thermostatic?
  • Is the shower floor waterproofed and flood tested before tile?
  • Will an access panel be left behind the valve for future service?
  • What happens if opening the wall reveals corroded supply lines or a failing drain?

Related Work Around A Bath Remodel

When the wall opens, it is the right time to address nearby lines. A worn fixture shutoff can lead to toilet repair and installation in the same room, and older supply lines may justify a look at home repipe service. If you suspect a hidden leak before demolition, leak testing can confirm it.
