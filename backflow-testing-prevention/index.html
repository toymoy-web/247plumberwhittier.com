Got A Backflow Test Notice From Your Water Provider?

A letter from your water provider asking for a backflow test can feel like a surprise bill. Backflow testing and prevention in Whittier keeps contaminated water from flowing backward into the public supply, and the notice means a device on your property needs checking.

Backflow happens when pressure drops or reverses and pulls water from a lawn sprinkler, a boiler, or a hose back toward the main. A backflow preventer stops it, and a test proves the preventer still works.

What The Rules Say

California's cross-connection control rules are in Title 17 of the California Code of Regulations, and water providers adopt programs that require testing certain devices on a schedule. Your provider decides which connections need annual testing and which form to submit.

Tests must be performed by a tester the provider recognizes, and the results go to the provider on its form. Ask the provider for its approved tester list and deadline before you book, so you do not pay for a test it will not accept.

Assemblies themselves are built to published standards, such as ASSE 1013 for reduced pressure assemblies, ASSE 1015 for double check assemblies, and ASSE 1020 for pressure vacuum breakers. Ask that any replacement appear on the approved list kept by the USC Foundation for Cross-Connection Control and Hydraulic Research.

Signs A Device Needs Attention

Water spitting from the relief port

A reduced pressure assembly that dumps water may have a fouled check valve.

Leaking at test cocks

Dripping fittings suggest worn seals.

Freeze or impact damage

A cracked body or bonnet needs replacement.

Missed annual tests

A device not tested on schedule can trigger notices or shutoff from the provider.

What To Do Before The Tester Arrives

A few minutes of preparation prevents a second trip.

  • Find your provider's notice and note the due date, the account number, and the device location.
  • Clear plants, boxes, and debris from around the device so the tester can reach the test ports.
  • Shut off the irrigation controller for the day, since water may be shut off briefly during the test.
  • Tell the dispatcher the make, size, and serial number if you can read them from the device tag.
  • Ask for the result in writing, and keep it with your other property records.
