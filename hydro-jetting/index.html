Snaked It And It Clogged Again A Few Weeks Later?

A drain that clears for a short time and then slows again, a cable that gets stuck partway down a line, or roots that grow back every season all suggest the pipe wall needs more than a hole punched through it. Hydro jetting in Whittier cleans the pipe itself.

What Hydro Jetting Does

A hydro jetter pushes water through a hose at high pressure and out of a nozzle with jets that point both forward and backward. The forward jets break up a blockage and the rear jets pull the hose down the line while scouring the pipe wall. Grease, soap scale, silt, and fine roots wash toward the cleanout instead of being pushed along.

A cable machine is fine for a hairball or a toy. Jetting earns its place on long runs, kitchen lines coated with grease, and mains where roots keep returning. The tool matters less than whether it is matched to the cause, which is why a camera look comes first. The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), requires cleanouts that give access for this kind of work.

Roots are a common story in this area. Many sewer laterals in Whittier's 1950s neighborhoods were clay, and clay joints give fine roots a place to start. Jetting can cut those roots back to the pipe wall, but the roots return unless the joint is repaired.

When Jetting Is The Wrong Tool

A good technician says no when the pipe cannot take it. These are the red flags:

Visible cracks or breaks

Camera footage showing a split pipe means pressure could push water into the soil and widen the damage.

A belly that holds water

A sagging section collects debris no matter how clean the pipe walls are, and the fix is structural.

Brittle old pipe

Corroded or thin-walled pipe may not tolerate full pressure and needs a gentler approach.

Offset joints

A joint that has slid out of line catches debris. It needs correcting, not cleaning.

Getting Ready For A Jetting Visit

A little preparation helps the crew work faster and keeps the property tidy.

  • Locate the cleanout and clear plants, bins, or vehicles from around it.
  • Gather any past inspection videos or invoices. They reveal what the line looked like before.
  • Tell the dispatcher about restaurants, laundry rooms, or garbage disposals on the line, since grease and lint change the plan.
  • Move rugs or valuables from the area near the lowest drain in case wastewater surges.
  • Ask what the technician will do if the camera shows damage, and if you will be called before anything changes.
